# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

A navigable index and quick overview of the project management processes used by OctoAcme.

## Project management process summary

OctoAcme uses a customer-first, iterative project lifecycle with clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and continuous learning. The process begins with initiation, where the team validates the business need, defines the objective and success metrics, identifies stakeholders, outlines milestones and risks, and decides whether an initiative should proceed to planning. This stage creates a shared understanding of the problem and sets the foundation for a realistic, value-driven plan.

Once the initiative is approved, the team moves into planning. The approved idea is translated into a prioritized, estimated backlog, with explicit acceptance criteria and a Definition of Done. Dependencies, risks, responsibilities, and release plans are also identified during this phase so the delivery team can move from general goals to actionable work. This creates alignment among stakeholders and reduces ambiguity during execution.

Execution and tracking focus on delivering small increments with visibility and control. The team manages work through a project board and pull request workflow, keeping changes small, reviewable, and connected to issue context and acceptance criteria. Quality is reinforced through CI, tests, security scanning, and manual QA where appropriate, while progress is tracked through metrics, demos, and risk monitoring. If blockers arise, they are escalated through clear levels from team-level triage to PM, Product Lead, and sponsor involvement when business impact requires it.

Risk management, communication, and release readiness are treated as continuous obligations rather than isolated activities. The team maintains a risk register, monitors dependencies, shares regular status updates, and uses standard communication templates for weekly reporting and incident updates. Before deployment, the team confirms acceptance criteria, CI and security checks, release notes, smoke testing, and rollback plans. After a sprint, release, or milestone, the team performs a retrospective to identify improvements, assign action items, and reinforce a culture of learning and incremental betterment.

The documentation also defines the responsibilities and communication patterns for developers, product managers, and project managers, so each role understands how to contribute to delivery, quality, and stakeholder alignment.
